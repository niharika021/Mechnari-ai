Mechnari.ai

A DFMEA platform that treats a manufacturer's own warranty history as the primary knowledge source for analysing a new part — and tells engineers what the review they just signed never checked.

Niharika Yadav · 10+ years mechanical engineering · Google Patchamomma 2026

§ 1

The problem is omission, not typing speed

Design FMEA is the mandatory instrument for catching how a part fails before it is built — AIAG-VDA in automotive, SAE ARP5580 in aerospace, ISO 14971 in medical devices. In practice it has three chronic faults.

  • Slow. A DFMEA workshop for one 10–14 part assembly package runs about a calendar week of cross-functional sessions; industry reports cite a comparable two to four weeks for a new part more broadly.
  • Inconsistent. Failure modes surface from whichever engineers happen to be in the room and what they happen to remember.
  • Disconnected from institutional memory. Warranty claims, 8D reports and prior DFMEA records sit in separate systems and rarely feed the next part's analysis.
From the job itself

Niharika Yadav, who is building this, has spent 10 years as a mechanical design engineer in agriculture — tractors, specifically. In that job, a DFMEA for one assembly or package (roughly 10–14 parts — a fuel routing package, a hydraulic package, that kind of grouping) takes about one calendar week, run as a cross-functional workshop with design, quality and manufacturing in the room together.

A full tractor has 1,000+ parts. At 10–14 parts per package, that is on the order of 70–100 of these week-long workshops somewhere in a single tractor program. Even with some running in parallel across different package teams, the total effort is on that scale — which is also the actual mechanism behind the second and third faults above: with that many workshops, run by different people at different points in the program, nothing keeps them consistent with each other or with what the last program already learned. It is not a training gap. It is a scale problem no manual process handles well.

The third fault is the expensive one. Speed is a commodity claim every competitor makes, it is unverifiable, and the part being sped up — filling in the table — is the part engineers least want automated. The defensible claim is the inverse: you have already seen this failure, and this DFMEA did not check for it. That is checkable against an 8D number, it survives an audit, and only a company's own history can produce it.

§ 2

Market

FMEA tooling is an established category with an active AI transition. Industry market reports place the FMEA software segment in the billions with a low-double-digit CAGR through the early 2030s, and every major report names AI/ML integration as the differentiator. A published Omnex case study on an automotive Tier-2 reports AI-assisted process FMEA generation cutting creation from three weeks to two hours while holding AIAG-VDA compliance.

Established players — Omnex FMEA AI Agent, Relyence FMEA, APIS IQ-FMEA — prove the demand and share one gap: they ship generic, industry-wide failure libraries. None treats a specific manufacturer's warranty and field data as a first-class, continuously learning input. Credible AI FMEA tools only became viable after 2023, so the category is early rather than saturated.

§ 3

What it does

An engineer describes a part — name, function, material, interface — without needing to know its failure modes. The system finds the closest historically similar parts, produces a grounded DFMEA with an evidence trail, and flags every failure mode known for that kind of part that has not been analysed.

Two properties make the output usable rather than merely plausible. Every row carries the 8D or warranty record it came from, so an engineer can check it and an auditor can trace it. And no number is ever produced by a language model — severity, occurrence, detection and Action Priority all come from deterministic lookups, which is what keeps the result defensible under IATF 16949.

Three views, because these are three different jobs

Drafting a DFMEA, auditing one, and reporting on program risk are not the same task and do not want the same screen. The application splits into three role views over one deterministic core.

Design engineer Draft from history Describe a part that need not exist yet; get candidate rows with the 8D records behind them, and — on every High row — the single change in Occurrence or Detection that would actually move its Action Priority, computed against the AP table rather than suggested
Quality engineer Audit, not drafting A review queue of submitted drafts, each linkable by URL; declined High rows are shown as considered-and-declined rather than missing. The same audit suite runs against parts already on file
Leadership Program rollup Coverage, open safety gaps by subsystem, and the backtest curve — the evidence for the spend, not row detail

The split is also what makes the declined-row trail possible: an engineer who leaves a High row out is recorded as having decided, not as having missed it, and the quality reviewer sees that distinction. That is the artefact an auditor asks for.

Domain note — why not RPN

AIAG-VDA dropped RPN in 2019 because multiplication misranks risk: S=9, O=2, D=2 scores 36 while S=4, O=3, D=4 scores 48, which says the safety-relevant failure matters less. Action Priority is a band lookup read severity-first — and a lookup table is more auditable than a product, because it cannot be argued with.

§ 5

Architecture

Five agents built on Google ADK 2.x. The division of labour is the architecture's whole point: agents read findings and write English, deterministic engines own every number. No agent holds a tool that could write a score — a test enforces it rather than a README asserting it.

Deterministic Semantic Retrieval TF-IDF cosine over the active BOM; finds the closest historical parts and infers the part type from them
Deterministic Failure RAG Grounds every mode in a real 8D or warranty record, never a guess
Deterministic Gap Detection Set difference: modes known for the part's type and family, minus the modes its DFMEA covers
Deterministic Risk Engine Severity from the effect registry, Occurrence from claims per 1,000 units, Action Priority from the AIAG-VDA band lookup
LLM · Gemini Mitigation Writer Turns findings into material, geometry and validation actions — prose only, constrained to the component's real material and medium

Behind them sit the knowledge base tables: a two-level part taxonomy, a failure effect registry holding one severity per effect organization-wide, a failure mode catalogue scoped to type or family, the warranty record, and the DFMEA worksheet on file. Every table is organization_id-ready, so the same schema scales from one company to multi-tenant SaaS without redesign.

The service boundary

The engines are Python modules with their own test suites, and they are reached two ways. api.py is a FastAPI wrapper that is deliberately thin: every route calls one of those modules and shapes its return value as JSON, and computes nothing itself. The Next.js frontend talks only to that boundary, calling the same modules in-process, unchanged. Neither frontend can produce a number the engines did not produce, because neither frontend contains the arithmetic.

One consequence is worth stating, because it was a bug before it was a principle: the Action Priority levers on each candidate row are computed server-side and sent with the row. Having the client fetch them per row put a loading state in front of exactly the High rows that most need the answer. The lever computation is a pure table lookup, so doing all of them inside the proposal costs nothing and removes the failure mode entirely.

Why the taxonomy has two levels

A failure mode attaches at the level it actually generalises to. Chafing wear-through is real for any flexible line, so it sits at family level; coking inside a PTFE lumen is only real downstream of an air compressor, so it sits at type level. The first build keyed everything to part type and produced a finding telling an engineer to check a wire conduit for park-brake binding. Inheritance has to be narrow enough to stay true, or the tool loses its reader on one screen.

§ 6

Build status — what is measured

A working v3 prototype runs the full pipeline end to end on a 50-part heavy tractor BOM. Every number below is reproducible from the repository; the harnesses that produce them are committed alongside the code.

The backtest

The warranty history is cut at a date. The system is given only what was known before it, and asked what it would have flagged on the failures that came after. Nothing after the cutoff is visible to it.

DFMEA on file caught 72% The manual baseline, on failures it could have anticipated
Mechnari would flag 98% +27 points, holding across five cutoffs
Failures newly caught 16 All 16 learned on a different part; 3 at severity 9+
Warranty claims behind them 228 Claim-weighted: 72% baseline against 99%
Why the numbers are not higher, and why that matters

Modes with no pre-cutoff record anywhere are counted as unknowable and excluded from both sides rather than scored as misses — 25 of 85 incidents at the reference cutoff. Nobody could have flagged them, and including them would quietly inflate the headline.

Mechnari does not score 100% either. Some failures cross the taxonomy — the same physics on a different kind of part — and those it cannot anticipate. A backtest that always scores perfectly is measuring its own construction, not the product.

Cold start — a part that does not exist yet

Each part is hidden from the corpus entirely — no DFMEA, no history of its own — and retrieval works from its written description alone. It surfaces 73% of the failure modes that really failed on it (82 of 112), in a shortlist averaging 19 candidates out of 71 catalogued modes.

Worked example: a never-before-seen EPDM Fuel Return Line, described in words only, retrieves three close fuel-hose analogues and proposes Fuel leakage at crimped ferrule joint at severity 9 — a mode proven on a hose retired in 2022 and cited to 8D-2023-0018. No part currently in the BOM would have surfaced it.

Retrieval quality, reported in full

Leave-one-out metricResultReading
Mode recall 76% The product metric — the share of truly applicable modes the proposal surfaces
True type among neighbours 72% The correct part type appears in the shortlist the engineer chooses from
Leading family correct 66% Family-scoped modes are the general lessons, so this carries weight
Leading type exactly right 46% Weak — and the reason the system proposes rather than decides

Top-1 type prediction plateaus near 50% on a 50-part corpus spread over 17 types, and no tuning fixes it — after holding a part out, some types have a single sibling left. So the agent does not assert a type. It proposes the modes for every kind of part among the neighbours, states its confidence, and asks an engineer to confirm, with an override that narrows the proposal once they do. That choice is worth 19 points of mode recall over winner-take-all.

§ 7

Known limitations

Stated here rather than discovered in review. Two are open items; two are properties of the current stage.

Action Priority table — partially verified

AIAG-VDA publishes three separate AP tables — DFMEA, PFMEA and FMEA-MSR — because Occurrence and Detection mean different things in each. The band structure here was checked against a primary source: a slide from Jochen Pfeufer, the VDA project lead who co-authored the AIAG-VDA alignment, presenting the actual DFMEA AP table at the SMMT AQMS conference (Nov 2018). That check caught a real structural error — Severity does not split into 5 bands, it splits into 4 (9–10 / 5–8 / 2–4 / 1) — and fixed it, along with 7 specific H/M/L cells now taken directly from that source.

The source itself is an "Extract" of a pre-publication draft, explicitly marked non-binding, so the remaining cells are still a best-effort completion pending the final 2019 handbook. Every cell is individually tagged verified, derived, or unconfirmed in risk_engine.py. The system still labels AP as provisional and keeps RPN alongside until every cell is checked.

  • The data is synthetic. Domain-correlated and internally consistent, but the backtest measures the system against generated warranty history. The harness is built to run unchanged against a pilot company's anonymised data — that substitution is the next real validation, not a rewrite.
  • TF-IDF is a floor, not a ceiling. At 50 parts it is the honest choice. Moving to Vertex AI embeddings replaces two functions and nothing downstream.
  • Effects are inherited with their origin. A mode carried to a new part keeps the effect it had on the part that taught it, so a coolant drain valve can inherit an AC valve's cab-climate effect. Re-evaluating effect per target part is the known fix.
